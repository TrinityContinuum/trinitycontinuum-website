Questions every community can ask before a data center is built

Data centers provide important services. Communities deserve understandable answers about water, electricity, cooling, noise and who pays for utility upgrades. Use this checklist to start a constructive conversation.

Discuss site-specific infrastructure optionsExplore data center environmental questions

Six questions worth asking publicly

1. Where will the water come from?

Request projected daily and annual withdrawals, peak-day demand, drought plans, water quality requirements and whether reclaimed or non-potable sources are feasible.

2. How will cooling work?

Ask whether cooling is evaporative, dry, liquid, hybrid or another design. Distinguish water circulated in a closed loop from water consumed through evaporation and discharge.

3. Who pays for electrical upgrades?

Ask for projected electrical demand, grid-interconnection needs, emergency backup plans and how infrastructure costs are allocated.

4. What happens during emergencies?

Review backup power, cooling continuity, fuel safety, utility coordination, water interruptions and emergency communications.

5. How will environmental impacts be measured?

Request baseline and ongoing measurements of water withdrawals and consumption, electrical demand, emissions, wastewater, noise and thermal discharge.

6. What benefits and tradeoffs are documented?

Ask about jobs, tax arrangements, infrastructure investments and community protections. Compare alternatives with transparent engineering and lifecycle assumptions.

Could integrated utilities improve the design?

Trinity Continuum is developing custom modular water, power and thermal infrastructure. A coordinated feasibility review may explore industrial water treatment and reuse, on-site generation, thermal recovery and resilient utility configurations. The best option depends on actual loads, local conditions, permits, fuel, economics and independent testing.

Our proposed platform is not proof of reduced water use, reduced emissions, certified potable water or an installed data-center solution. We encourage developers and communities to evaluate alternatives with measurable baseline-versus-proposed performance.
