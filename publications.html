Data Centers Need a New Infrastructure Playbook.

Mobile power, water treatment and thermal integration can provide a modular complement to conventional grid connections, water networks and permanent utility expansion.

Trinity Continuum is developing an integrated trailer-based utility platform intended to help industrial sites evaluate alternatives to relying solely on large, fixed infrastructure projects. For data centers, the opportunity is to evaluate power, water and thermal needs as one coordinated system—not three disconnected construction projects.

The challenge

Power growth. Cooling demand. Local constraints.

Electricity

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's 2026 update estimates U.S. data centers could account for 9.5%–15.3% of national electricity use by 2030 across its modeled scenarios. These are projections, not guaranteed outcomes.

Water

Cooling designs can draw on municipal, reclaimed or other water supplies. Treatment requirements, withdrawal and consumption vary substantially by site and cooling architecture.

Construction

Grid upgrades, transformers, permitting, pipelines and heat-rejection equipment can shape project schedules and long-term capital commitments. A modular approach may help stage capacity where feasible.

Our proposed approach

Three utilities. One coordinated deployment strategy.

Modular power

Evaluate on-site electrical generation and island-capable controls for eligible loads. Availability, fuel supply, emissions permits, redundancy and utility interconnection remain project-specific.

Conditioned process water

Evaluate treatment and polishing tailored to actual cooling and process-water specifications. Ultra-pure water is not automatically the right choice for every cooling loop.

Thermal integration

Examine useful heat recovery and engineered heat rejection together. Every installation still needs a complete heat-sink, cooling and water-balance design.

Our current six-trailer flagship uses a 30-million-gallon-per-year modeled water-production planning target at 8,000 annual operating hours. Electrical best-case modeling is slightly below 1 MW; neither figure is a guaranteed field-delivered performance rating. Large data centers would require a separately engineered multi-module capacity and reliability assessment.

Responsible comparison

Not every data center should use the same infrastructure.

Permanent utility systems remain essential in many locations. The right comparison is lifecycle cost, water consumption, reliability, emissions, permitting, construction schedule and maintainability—not an unsupported claim that all fixed infrastructure is detrimental. Mobile systems also require fuel logistics, noise controls, site space, equipment service and end-of-life planning.

What must be proven for a deployment

Independent water testing; verified net electrical and thermal balances; heat rejection; equipment certification; uptime and redundancy; emissions and noise; site permits; fuel and feedwater security; and a transparent lifecycle financial model.
