What if industrial byproducts became useful resources?

Gas that might otherwise be flared, treated wastewater and saline water can present opportunities for carefully engineered recovery and reuse. Trinity Continuum is developing integrated water, power and thermal systems designed around site-specific conditions.

Three problems worth engineering around

Otherwise flared gas

Some industrial sites flare gas because collection, treatment, transport or use is impractical under current conditions. Potential utilization requires gas composition testing, contaminant removal where appropriate, reliable supply, combustion engineering, emissions controls, safety systems and permits. Capturing gas does not automatically establish lower total emissions.

Recycled wastewater

Wastewater reuse can reduce the need for some freshwater withdrawals where treatment and end-use requirements align. Feedwater variability, pretreatment, contaminants, membranes or distillation, residuals management and independent product-water testing must be evaluated.

Saline and challenging water

Brackish water, saline water and other difficult feeds may be candidates for tailored treatment. Feasibility depends on salinity, chemistry, energy demand, corrosion, scaling, discharge permissions and the required finished-water quality.

Custom configurations—not one-size-fits-all claims

Trinity Continuum's proposed modular platform combines industrial water treatment, on-site electrical generation and thermal-energy recovery. Our engineering approach targets long service life, with a 30-year design-life objective subject to materials qualification, inspection, maintenance, replacement schedules and validation.

No claim is made that any gas stream is safe for use without treatment, that every wastewater or saline feed can meet a specified grade, that a 30-year lifespan has been demonstrated, or that emissions and water savings are guaranteed. Each site requires an engineering and regulatory feasibility review.

What information helps us assess a possible project?

For gas: composition, flow, pressure, contaminants, existing flare permits and desired use. For water: source, laboratory analysis, volume, target quality, disposal options and permits. For the site: power and heat loads, location, existing utilities, operating schedule and project objectives.
